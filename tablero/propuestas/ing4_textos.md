## James Salter's Days in Film · Examen 1, Parte 5, pp. 10–11 (cuaderno: QA pregunta 18, QC 17, QD 20)

> Lea el artículo y luego responda las preguntas. En las preguntas 17 - 23, marque A, B o C.

**JAMES SALTER'S DAYS IN FILM**

James Salter was a pilot in the United States Air Force. He abandoned the military profession in
1957 after the publication of his first novel, *The Hunters*. He is best known as a novelist, but
during the sixties and seventies, he worked in film making. Salter made documentaries, wrote scripts
for films, and even was the director of a film called *Three*, starring Charlotte Rampling and Sam
Waterston.

*Burning the Days*, a short story published in *The New Yorker* in 1997, where Salter tells the story
of his life, was adapted into a film called *Passionate Falsehoods*.

Salter's time in the film world is both good and bad. In Rome, he met directors and stars. In New
York, he explored the city with Robert Redford and enjoyed being famous. Deborah Treisman, and
Michael Agger have talked about Salter. Nick Paumgarten in *The Last Book*, describes Salter's
opinion about his film career:

"Of sixteen scripts for movies, only four were popular. There was money, attractive women, and
entrance into rooms where there were stories more for the dinner table than for the page". Salter
thought he was wasting his time.

Perhaps he wasted his time in a larger artistic way, but it still makes for attractive reading.
*The Last Book* is available to everyone in online stores.

| 17 | James Salter played an important part in the making of movies | from 1960 to 1979. | in 1957. | after 1997. | **A** |
| 18 | Passionate Falsehoods is | a newspaper. | a play. | a movie. | **C** |

Lo que el profesor debe hacer notar:

- **17 · A.** «the sixties and seventies» son los años 1960 a 1979. Las otras dos fechas están en el
  texto pero son de otra cosa: 1957 (deja el ejército), 1997 (sale el cuento). Cada cifra tiene dueño.
- **18 · C.** El ancla es el título. «was adapted into a film»: *film* = *movie*. *The New Yorker*
  es la revista donde salió el cuento, no lo que es *Passionate Falsehoods*.
- **20 · C.** «Nick Paumgarten in *The Last Book*, describes…»: el libro es suyo. Los tres nombres de
  las opciones están en el texto; solo uno escribió el libro.
- Las preguntas 19, 21, 22 y 23 de este artículo no se usan en el cuaderno.

## My Artistic Adventure · Examen 2, Parte 5, pp. 18–19 (cuaderno: QB pregunta 18)

> Lea el artículo y luego responda las preguntas. En las preguntas 15 - 19, marque A, B o C.

**MY ARTISTIC ADVENTURE**

When I was a child in Ireland, I went to bed late, and I could not sleep past six. One morning, I
found a Spanish television show with a French man who was painting a beautiful countryside with oil
paints.

Right then, I began watching this show. With his quiet voice he explained his natural way of
painting, and he quickly made trees and rivers, simply by moving his hand across the canvas. He made
it look simple and easy, so I thought I could do it by myself.

Every Sunday morning, I would try to paint what he was painting on the screen, but I needed oil
paints to make my paintings as beautiful as his. I could not buy oil paints; I was only seven, and my
parents would not buy them for me. Then I decided to use my mother's cosmetics and paper for my
painting. I was ready to paint my first picture.

After my mum saw what I did with her things, I was told I could not paint again. This put an end to
my idea of using food the next time to make a picture. After a while, I stopped watching the French
painter. This put and end to my dream of becoming an artist.

| Pregunta | Tronco | A | B | C | Clave |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 | She could not get oil paints because she | didn't speak Spanish. | was too young. | always watched TV. | **B** |

Lo que el profesor debe hacer notar:

- **18 · B.** «I was only seven» dicho con otras palabras: *was too young*. El programa era español
  (dato real del texto), pero no es la razón. Es la estrategia 3 (lo dice con otras palabras).
- El original trae la errata «This put and end» (por «an end»): se copió tal cual.
